# Client documents, PDF and copying

Video: **Help → Client documents, PDF and copying** in the app.

## Discharge instructions and client summaries

Under a note in Review, choose **Discharge instructions** or **Client summary**:
- They're written in plain language for the pet's owner, with medications given exactly as said.
- They follow your note, including any corrections you made by hand.
- You can make them before or after the note is signed.
- They open in a side panel. Read them through, then **Copy** them or **Save PDF**. **Draft again** writes a fresh version.
- **Language** in the side panel writes the document in the client's language (English, French, Spanish, German, Portuguese, Italian or Dutch), whatever language your note is in. Medications and numbers are copied unchanged and checked again.
- Two more client documents: **Estimate explanation** (the plan and its costs, prices copied exactly) and **Euthanasia aftercare** (arrangements and support, gently worded).

## Copy the note into your PIMS

**Copy note** puts the whole note, with the patient, reason, date and signature, on the clipboard. Each heading also has its own **Copy**. In **Settings → PDFs and copying**, choose how it pastes:

| Copy note as | Best for |
|---|---|
| Plain text | Most PIMS note boxes |
| Formatted text | PIMS with a rich text editor (headings and bullet lists paste as formatting) |
| Markdown | Tools that understand Markdown |

**Include transcript line numbers when copying** adds the line references, such as [4, 7], if your clinic wants them in the record.

## Save a PDF

**Save PDF** (in Review's **More** menu, or in a client document's panel) saves the note, or a client document, as a PDF. It includes:
- your clinic's name and address from Settings, as a letterhead;
- the patient, reason for visit and date;
- the signature, or a clear "draft" line if it isn't signed;
- every heading, with "Not discussed" where nothing was said;
- addenda and page numbers.

Choose Letter or A4 in Settings. The PDF's own words (such as "Signed by" and "Page 1 of 2") follow the note language.

PDFs and copies are the only way clinical text leaves OpenBark unencrypted, so keep them as carefully as any medical record.
