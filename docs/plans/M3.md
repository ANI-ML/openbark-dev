## Goal

A clinician records a consult on their iPhone and the phone transcribes it. The transcript then appears on every computer they're signed in to, where they draft, review and sign the note. The recording stays on the phone. The server only ever holds ciphertext. OpenBark keeps working with no account.

### Phase 1: S1 iOS audio check (MAC, APPLE)

- A throwaway Tauri iOS build, built, signed and uploaded to TestFlight by the S1 job on a GitHub macOS runner. No owner Mac.
- The CI job runs what a simulator can check: the app builds with sherpa-onnx, the WebView loads, and recording to an encrypted file works with a test signal.
- The owner installs the TestFlight build on their iPhone and follows a scripted checklist in `docs/runbooks/ios.md`. The app records its own measurements (lost frames, interruptions, real-time factor and memory) and shows a summary the owner can copy back.
- S1 criteria A1 to A4 are tested. Parakeet's memory use and real-time factor are measured.
- The result goes into ADR-0002 (the iOS part) and ADR-0018.

Prerequisite: the owner has added the signing secrets to GitHub and created the app record.

Acceptance:
- **A1:** at most 0.5% of audio frames lost across locking, backgrounding and an incoming call.
- **A2:** the app is never killed in 15 minutes, in 3 of 3 runs.
- **A3:** no blank WebView, in 3 of 3 runs.
- **A4:** live transcription at a real-time factor of 0.5 or less.
- If A1 to A3 fail, phase 4 switches to the SwiftUI and UniFFI shell, which adds about 2 weeks.

### Phase 2: encrypted sync between computers (WIN, SB-DEV)

Deliverables:
- **The `engine/os-sync` crate:** keys, the OBS1 format, mapping, merging, the outbox, the Supabase REST client and the plaintext guard. The outbox refuses audio files.
- **`supabase/` migrations:**
  - the `sync` schema and its RLS policies, with blobs stored as ciphertext in Postgres (no Storage bucket);
  - `device_ok()`, `push` and `pull`;
  - the Edge Functions `revoke-device`, `delete-account` and `purge`.
- **Settings > Sync**, on the desktop and in web mode:
  - turn on sync and sign in with a code;
  - a plain statement that sync carries transcripts and notes, and recordings stay on the device;
  - the Recovery Kit, with its two-word check;
  - add a device by comparing a 6-word safety code. The QR code is shown too, ready for the phone;
  - the device list, and Remove, which rotates the keys;
  - Turn off sync and Delete my account.
- **What syncs:** visit headers, events, transcripts, runs and patients. Never audio. Desktops poll every 30 s, and each visit shows its sync status.
- **Visits from another device:** the evidence link shows the transcript line and its time, and says where the audio is ("The audio is on Anna's iPhone").
- **Which visits sync,** with different rules for single-user mode and team mode (ADR-0017 section 3).
- **`docs/network.md`:** rows R6 and R7, and the updated source check.
- **Release:** labelled **Preview**, and used on test data only until phase 3 passes.

Acceptance, all on Windows:
- **Setup.** Two isolated demo servers on ports 4321 and 4325. Each has its own `OPENBARK_SESSIONS_DIR`, `OPENBARK_DATA_KEY` and app data folder, and both run against a local Supabase.
- **Basic sync.** A visit imported and transcribed on server A appears on B as Transcribed within 60 s, without its audio. Once it is drafted and signed on B, A shows the signed note.
- **Offline conflicts.** With both servers offline, A edits the patient while B drafts and signs. After they reconnect:
  - both reach the same state;
  - no draft is lost;
  - a conflicting draft is offered as an addendum.
- **Removing a device.** After B is removed from A:
  - B's next request is refused at once, not when its token expires;
  - after key rotation, B can't open new objects;
  - B erases its synced copies when it next connects.
- **Recovery.** A new device restores everything from the Recovery Kit alone, even after a rotation.
- **Plaintext test.** Every request body sent to REST is OBS1 or OBA1. No fixture patient name appears in any body, or in any stored row. No request goes to Storage, and no audio leaves the device.
- **RLS tests.** Account X can't read account Y's data. A pending device can read only its own envelopes.
- **Fuzzing.** The OBS1 parser is fuzzed with no crash.
- **Sync off.** The offline end-to-end test passes and no new connections appear.
- **Real server.** The same suite passes once against the development project, `nmecovtmijnfnjuadicp`, with fixture data only.

### Phase 4: iPhone app v1 (MAC, APPLE, WIN)

Steps:
1. **Phone layout in React,** built at phone width in a desktop browser on Windows: Record, Today, Visit, Patients and Settings.
2. **The `os-ios` plugin:** the audio session, interruptions, the idle timer, background tasks and Face ID.
3. **Transcription on the phone:** turn on `stt` with the sherpa-onnx xcframework (shared linking). Parakeet and Silero VAD download on Wi-Fi, and transcription runs during recording.
4. **First feature TestFlight build:** recording and transcription on the phone, with no account.
5. **Sync:** transcripts and notes upload through `os-sync`. The recording stays on the phone.
6. **Pairing and account:** scan a QR code to approve a computer, or be approved by one. Account deletion in the app.
7. **Builds:** every build is made, signed and uploaded by the TestFlight job on a GitHub macOS runner.

Acceptance:
- S1 A1 to A4 pass again in the real app on the owner's iPhone.
- **Full flow.** A 15-minute recording runs with the screen locked, the app in the background and an incoming call.
  - The final transcript is on the phone within 2 minutes of Stop, with the app in the foreground. That target is revised after S1.
  - The visit appears as Transcribed on a signed-in PC within 60 s of the phone having a connection. The PC shows the transcript and its times, and says the audio is on the phone.
  - Once the visit is drafted and signed on the PC, the signed note is readable on the phone.
- **No account.** The phone records, transcribes and lists visits with no account. This is the App Review path.
- **Airplane mode.** The phone records and transcribes, and uploads the transcript and notes when the connection returns.
- **Before the models download.** A recording is kept as "Not transcribed yet", is transcribed once the models arrive, and only then syncs.
- **On the phone.** The app's container holds no plaintext file. A CI job checks this in the simulator by scanning the app's container after a scripted recording.
- **On the server.** The development project's database contains no fixture patient name and no audio.
- **TestFlight.** The owner and one other tester install an internal build.
- **Pilot.** A pilot with real clinic data starts only after phase 3 passes.

Dropped from this phase by owner decision 2 (each would need a new decision): the audio format eval (AAC or FLAC), Sync recordings, audio uploads and downloads, cloud and phone audio retention, record-only phones and processing jobs.

Acceptance:

- A device that hasn't connected for the lease period locks its synced copies and keeps its own visits and recordings.
- A template edited on one computer appears on another, and the old version is kept.
- Delete everywhere, by a veterinarian, removes the visit from every device on its next pull and is recorded in the audit log. A technician can't start it.

## Risks

- **macOS CI minutes and owner device checks are the bottleneck.** Minutes count ten times on the private repo, and every on-device check waits for the owner to install a TestFlight build. Mitigation: Windows-first phasing, `scripts/ios-check.sh` before any macOS run, caching, signed builds only on tags or by hand, and scripted runbooks for the owner.
- **Signing without a Mac.** Making the certificate and `.p12` on Windows is less common. Mitigation: OpenSSL steps in the runbook, tested once before phase 1.
- **Tauri background audio on iOS is unproven** (tauri#12523). Mitigation: phase 1 decides before any feature work, and the SwiftUI shell is the fallback.
- **One plaintext upload would break the promise.** Mitigation: the plaintext guard, the outbox refusing audio, the request-body test, the source check in network.md, and the review gate before real data.
- **Audio isn't available on other devices.** A vet at a computer can't replay the phone's recording to check a line. Mitigation: the evidence link shows the transcript line and its time and says which device holds the audio. Bringing audio to computers would need a new decision.
- **Recordings fill the phone,** because they never upload. Mitigation: free-space checks before recording, storage shown in Settings, and manual deletion. Automatic clean-up would need a new decision.
- **Merge bugs could touch signed records.** Mitigation: immutable runs, one-way signing, visible separate drafts, and property tests with offline devices and clock skew.
- **Losing every device and the Recovery Kit loses the cloud copy.** Mitigation: the two-word check, one printed sheet, and the local copies remaining the record.
- **iOS allows only about 30 s of work after Stop.** Mitigation: transcription during recording, small transcript and note uploads, and resuming in the foreground.
- **Sign-in emails could be delayed or capped.** Mitigation: SendGrid as custom SMTP from the start, and TOTP.
- **The Supabase project may be in the wrong region.** Mitigation: check it now. If it isn't `ca-central-1`, it stays for development and the pilot uses a new project.
- **App Review could raise questions** about background audio, medical framing, account deletion and export compliance. Mitigation: internal TestFlight for the pilot, and the review material prepared in phase 6.
- **Metadata leaks:** visit volume, working hours and a loose hint of consult length. Mitigation: padding, and saying so plainly.
- **On a shared computer, a PIN is not a cryptographic boundary.** Mitigation: documenting it. Real separation comes with the Hub (M4) or separate OS accounts.
- **Supabase could change its prices, or lock us in.** Mitigation: plain REST, migrations in the repo, ordinary Postgres rows, and a self-hostable server.
